🌍 Top 10 Obscure Costco Facts

Nov 10, 2026

1. 🇺🇸 Costco's oldest store sits in Howard Hughes's old hangars

The oldest store in the Costco family isn't in Seattle. It's on Morena Boulevard in San Diego, where Price Club opened on July 12, 1976, inside a row of old airplane hangars once owned by billionaire pilot Howard Hughes.

At first, only businesses could shop there. You had to show a resale certificate or a professional license and pay $25 a year.

Price Club merged with Costco in 1993. That first building is now Costco Warehouse #401, and it's still open 50 years later.

Think of it as buying paper towels in a famous billionaire's old garage.

2. 🇺🇸 Costco's family tree starts with a store only for government workers

In 1954, Sol Price, the man who later started Price Club, opened FedMart in an empty San Diego warehouse. Only government workers could shop there, and a family membership cost just $2.

Its first shelves held a weird mix: jewelry, furniture and liquor, because those were what his law clients sold. It still made $4.5 million in its first year, four times what the owners expected. Costco co-founder Jim Sinegal started there in 1955 as a grocery bagger.

And here's the twist: Walmart founder Sam Walton wrote that he borrowed more ideas from Sol Price than from anyone, and he liked the name "Wal-Mart" because he really liked "FedMart."

In other words, the "Mart" in Walmart comes from Costco's great-grandpa.

3. 🥜 Costco sells more than half of the world's cashews

As of 2025, Costco sold more than half of all the cashews in the world. Its buying choices affect over a million cashew farmers in Africa.

Cashews grow in a strange way. Each nut hangs off the bottom of a "cashew apple," a soft red or yellow fruit. The shell and raw seed are poisonous, so the nut has to be shelled and roasted or steamed before it's safe to eat.

The world harvested 3.9 million tons of cashews in 2023, led by Ivory Coast and India.

Basically, when Costco goes cashew shopping, it shops for half the planet.

4. ⌚ A tiny globe on a watch took Costco to the Supreme Court

Swiss watchmaker Omega put a small copyrighted globe drawing on the back of its watches. Costco bought real Omega watches that had been sold abroad, passed through middlemen, and ended up in its stores at a lower price.

Omega didn't say the watches were fake. It sued over the little globe, saying Costco had imported copyrighted art without permission. An appeals court agreed in 2008. The Supreme Court then tied 4–4 in 2010, because Justice Kagan sat out, so Omega's win stood.

But the case went back to a lower court, and the judge flipped it: using a copyrighted drawing to control who sells a watch was misusing copyright. In 2015, an appeals court agreed and made Omega pay Costco's lawyer fees.

It's like putting a sticker on your bike so nobody else can ever resell it.

5. ☀️ Costco turns its lights off when the sun comes out

Most Costco stores have several skylights in the roof. Electronic light meters measure how much sunlight is coming in, then switch off a matching share of the lights.

On a normal sunny day, the middle of the store often has no indoor lights on at all.

It's part of how tight Costco runs. Its profit is about 2 cents on every dollar, and it has no public relations department and buys no outside ads.

Think of it as a dimmer switch controlled by the sky.

6. 🏢 Costco's CEO works in a cubicle

At Costco's headquarters in Issaquah, Washington, all 7,000 office workers sit in cubicles, including the CEO. His is just slightly bigger.

Bosses aren't supposed to stay at their desks anyway. They're expected to spend most of their time visiting stores, and the CEO tries to visit as many as he can around the world every 18 to 24 months. The headquarters was even built next to a Costco store so buyers can walk over and check what's selling.

The current CEO, Ron Vachris, started in 1982 driving a forklift at a Price Club in Arizona.

Basically, the top job at Costco comes with a slightly bigger box.

7. 🇮🇸 In Iceland, the Costco food court serves fish stew

Costco's food courts look the same everywhere, but the menu changes by country. At Iceland's only Costco, you can get plokkfiskur, a traditional stew of flaked white fish mashed with potatoes, onion and a creamy sauce.

Canada gets poutine (fries with cheese curds and gravy). Australia gets meat pies, and the UK gets jacket potatoes, which are baked potatoes. Japan, South Korea and Taiwan get clam chowder, and Mexico gets pizza topped with al pastor taco meat.

It's like a band on world tour that plays a local song in every city.

8. 🇰🇷 The world's busiest Costco is in Seoul

Costco has more than 600 stores in the United States, but its top seller isn't one of them. In 2018, the Yangjae store in Seoul, South Korea, sold more than any other Costco on Earth.

Second place went to Taichung, Taiwan, which also had more members than any other Costco. Taiwan has only 14 stores, yet three of them made the world's top 10 for sales.

In other words, the busiest Costco is across the Pacific Ocean from where the chain began.

9. 🍷 Costco spent $22 million to end Washington's state-run liquor stores

In Costco's home state of Washington, hard liquor used to be sold only through state-owned and state-licensed liquor stores. Stores also had to buy wine through the state system. Costco sued over the wine rule and lost in 2006.

So it tried another way. It spent over $22 million backing Initiative 1183, a vote that let citizens change the law. The other side spent $11.75 million, which made it the most expensive ballot fight in Washington's history.

On November 8, 2011, it passed with 60% of the vote, and the state's control of liquor sales ended.

Think of it as a store rewriting the rules so it could fill its own shelves.

10. 🥤 Costco once stopped selling Coca-Cola over price

Costco only carries about 3,700 different products. A Walmart Supercenter carries about 140,000, nearly 40 times more. So if a brand's price is too high, Costco can simply drop it.

In November 2009, it did that to Coca-Cola, after Coke refused to lower its wholesale prices. Coke was back on the shelves the next month.

Then in 2013, Coke raised its prices, and US food courts swapped every Coke fountain drink for Pepsi. Coke didn't return until warehouses switched back in July 2025.

Even Coca-Cola has to pass Costco's price check.

Fun fact

In 2002, Costco tried a grocery-only store called Costco Fresh in Bellevue, Washington, with a logo that had a fruit stem growing out of the first "o." It was dropped the next year. Costco still wanted a regular store on that land until 2008, when it gave up because local rules would have made it "daylight" a creek buried underground, which means digging it up and bringing it back into the open air. Many cities hide old streams under their streets, and some are now bringing them back to the surface.
