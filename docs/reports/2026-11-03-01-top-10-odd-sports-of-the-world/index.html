🌍 Top 10 Odd Sports of the World

Nov 3, 2026

1. 🇮🇳 Kabaddi raiders must chant "kabaddi" on one breath

Kabaddi comes from India. Two teams of seven face off on a court. One player, the raider, runs into the other team's half, tries to tag as many defenders as possible, and then has to get back home without being tackled.

Here's the twist: the whole time, the raider keeps chanting "kabaddi, kabaddi, kabaddi." The chant proves to the referees that the raid is happening on a single breath. Stop chanting, and the other team gets a point.

A raid often lasts 30 seconds at most. Every defender tagged is a point for the raider's team, but if the defenders pin the raider down first, they score instead.

Basically, it's tag where you're not allowed to breathe in.

2. 🇲🇾 Sepak takraw is volleyball where you can't use your hands

Sepak takraw is played on a court like a badminton court, with a net across the middle. Teams of two to four players have to get the ball over using only their feet, knees, shoulders, chest and head.

The name is a deal between two countries. "Sepak" is Malay for kick, and "takraw" is Thai for a woven rattan ball. Officials from Malaysia and Thailand settled on the name in Kuala Lumpur in 1960, and today it counts as Malaysia's national sport.

The ball is hollow, woven with 12 holes, and light: 170 to 180 grams for men, about as much as an apple. That's light enough to flick high over the net with a kick.

It's volleyball played by soccer players.

3. 🇩🇪 Chess boxing makes you punch, then think, then punch again

In chess boxing, two fighters take turns: three minutes of speed chess, then three minutes of boxing, then back to chess. A match has up to 11 rounds, six of chess and five of boxing, and the same chess game carries on across all of them.

You win by checkmate or by knockout, whichever comes first. For every chess round, a platform with the table, board and chairs is lowered into the boxing ring from the ceiling.

The idea came from a 1992 French comic by Enki Bilal. Dutch artist Iepe Rubingh turned it into a real sport, and the first competition was held in Berlin in 2003.

Think of it as a math test in the middle of a fistfight.

4. 🇫🇮 In wife carrying, you race with your partner hanging upside down

Wife carrying started in 1992 in Sonkajärvi, a small town in Finland. A runner carries a teammate along an obstacle track as fast as possible. Most teams are a man carrying a woman, but in most races there are no rules about gender or marriage.

You can use a piggyback or carry them over one shoulder. The strangest hold is the "Estonian style": the partner hangs upside down on the runner's back, with their legs wrapped over the runner's shoulders and neck.

At the World Championships in Sonkajärvi, the track is 253.5 meters long, about two and a half soccer fields. The person being carried must weigh at least 49 kg (108 lb). Anyone lighter wears a heavy backpack to make up the difference.

It's a piggyback race with a rulebook.

5. 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 Underwater hockey is played on the bottom of a pool

Players wear a diving mask, snorkel and fins, and use a short stick to push a puck along the bottom of a swimming pool into the other team's goal. No scuba tanks are allowed, so everyone has to hold their breath every time they dive down.

The puck is made of lead and weighs 1.3 to 1.5 kg, about as much as a big bottle of water, so it stays on the pool floor. A 2023 study found players stay under for about 12 seconds at a time while they have the puck, then pop up for air.

It was invented in Portsmouth, England, in 1954 by Alan Blake, to keep his diving club busy in winter, when the sea was too cold. He called it Octopush, for its eight-player teams and the stick, called a "pusher."

In other words, it's hockey where you have to keep coming up for air.

6. 🇦🇷 Argentina's national sport, pato, was once played with a live duck

Pato means "duck" in Spanish. In the early games, written about since 1610, riders on horseback fought over a live duck in a basket. The field could stretch all the way between two neighboring ranches, and the first team to carry the duck back to its own ranch house won.

The game was so rough that it was banned again and again. Riders got trampled, and fights broke out in the middle of play.

In the 1930s a ranch owner wrote modern rules, and the duck became a leather ball with six handles. Teams of four throw it through an upright ring on top of a pole 2.4 meters high. The rider holding the ball must stretch their arm out so rivals get a chance to grab it. In 1953 Argentina made pato its national sport.

Think of it as basketball on horseback.

7. 🇮🇹 Calcio storico is Florence's old football where wrestling is allowed

Every June, teams from four parts of Florence, Italy, play in Piazza Santa Croce, a big square turned into a sand-covered field. Each team has 27 players, and nobody can be swapped in, not even for an injured player.

The goal is to get the ball into the net at the other end, by almost any means. Tackling, wrestling and even punching are allowed. Kicks to the head and sucker punches are banned, and two players can't gang up on one.

A small cannon fires to start the game and again after every goal. In a famous match on 17 February 1530, Florence played while its city was surrounded by the emperor's army, just to show it wasn't scared.

Basically, it's soccer, rugby and wrestling all at once.

8. 🤸 Bossaball is volleyball on a bouncy castle with trampolines

Bossaball is played on an inflatable court with a trampoline on each side of the net. Teams have four players, and one of them bounces on the trampoline to get high enough to spike the ball down on the other side.

You can hit the ball with your hands like in volleyball, or with your feet, chest and head like in soccer. A team gets up to five touches, and one of them has to be a soccer-style touch.

Soccer-style hits score more. Land the ball on the other team's trampoline with a kick or a header and you get 5 points, while a plain volleyball hit onto their court gets just 1. Filip Eyckmans came up with the sport in 2004, naming it after bossa nova, a style of Brazilian music.

Imagine volleyball in a bounce house.

9. 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 Toe wrestling is arm wrestling with your bare feet

Two players lock toes and each try to push the other's foot over to the side of a small stand called the "toedium," a mix of "toe" and "stadium." It's just like arm wrestling, but with feet. Players switch feet each round: right, then left, then right again if needed.

It began in 1974 in a pub in Staffordshire, England. Some drinkers were fed up with the World Cup and wanted a sport Britain would win. The very next year, a Canadian won.

Before each match, referees check feet for warts and infections. The biggest champion, Alan "Nasty" Nash, has won at least 14 times and broken at least four toes along the way.

It's thumb wrestling, but for feet.

10. 🇺🇸 Quadball is Quidditch with a pipe between your legs

Quadball started as "muggle Quidditch," copying the flying sport from the Harry Potter books. The version that grew into today's game was created in 2005 at Middlebury College in the US. Every player has to keep a stick, usually a plastic pipe about 1 meter long, between their legs the whole game, like a broom.

Chasers score 10 points by throwing a slightly deflated volleyball through one of three hoops. Beaters throw dodgeballs at them: get hit, and you must hop off your "broom" and run back to touch your own hoops.

Since nobody can chase a flying golden ball, after 20 minutes a referee dressed in yellow runs onto the field with a tennis ball in a long cloth tube hanging from their shorts. Grab that "flag" and your team gets 30 points. The sport was renamed quadball in 2022.

In other words, it's tag, dodgeball and handball while riding a pretend broom.

Fun fact

Long before chess boxing, the codebreaker Alan Turing invented his own sporty kind of chess. In "round-the-house chess," one player makes a move and then runs around the house, and the other player has to make their move before the runner gets back. Chess has many strange cousins like this, from games on three-dimensional boards to versions with extra pieces. Weird variants of chess could be a fun theme to explore next.
