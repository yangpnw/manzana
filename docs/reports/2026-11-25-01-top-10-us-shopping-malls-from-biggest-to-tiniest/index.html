🌍 Top 10 US Shopping Malls, from Biggest to Tiniest

Nov 25, 2026

1. 🥶 Mall of America has no heaters in its halls

The Mall of America in Bloomington, Minnesota, is the biggest mall in the US: about 5.7 million square feet. A newspaper once said seven Yankee Stadiums could fit inside.

Minnesota winters are freezing, but the mall's walkways have no heaters. Only the entrances and some underground areas are heated. Sunlight comes in through skylights, and the rest of the warmth comes from lights, gadgets and all the people walking around.

The mall sits where a baseball stadium used to be. A plaque in the floor of the indoor theme park marks the old home plate. High up on a wall, a single red stadium seat is bolted where Harmon Killebrew's 520-foot home run landed in 1967, almost one and a half football fields away.

Basically, the shoppers are the heaters.

2. 🎢 New Jersey's American Dream took 15 years to open

American Dream in East Rutherford, New Jersey, is the second-biggest mall in the US, at about 3 million square feet. Builders broke ground in 2004, and it finally opened in 2019. In between, it went through several owners and stopped and restarted again and again.

The half-built building was painted in loud colors you could see from the highway. In 2011, New Jersey's governor, Chris Christie, called it "an offense to the eyes" and the ugliest building in New Jersey, "and maybe America." In 2019, it was finally painted white.

Earlier that year, in February 2011, a record month of snow hit. Workers were trying to melt snow off the roof of the mall's indoor ski slope, and built-up ice made part of a wall collapse. Outdoor snow damaged the building made for indoor snow.

In other words, the slowest mall in America also had the worst luck with weather.

3. 🫘 California's biggest mall grew in a lima bean field

South Coast Plaza in Costa Mesa is California's biggest mall. The Segerstrom family built it in one of their own lima bean fields, in fast-growing Orange County, and it opened in 1967.

Unlike most big malls, it was never sold to a big investment company. The same family still owns it.

And it sells a lot. Its stores bring in roughly $2.5 billion a year, about $7 million every single day, which makes it the highest-earning shopping center in the US.

Basically, the bean field became a money field.

4. 🛝 Florida's biggest mall has a slide that counts as art

Aventura Mall, near Miami, is the biggest mall in Florida and the fifth-biggest in the US. It shows more than 25 big art pieces around the property.

The most famous one is the Aventura Slide Tower, a 93-foot spiral slide by the artist Carsten Höller. That's about as tall as a 9-story building. It's an artwork you're allowed to play on.

The mall also has a playground built as art and a three-level Apple Store. Readers of USA Today voted it the best mall in the US in both 2025 and 2026.

Think of it as a museum where you can go down the sculpture.

5. 🎠 New York's biggest mall was built on a junkyard

Destiny USA in Syracuse is the biggest mall in New York State. Before it was built, the land was a junkyard called Marley Scrap Yard, surrounded by blocks of oil tanks people called "Oil City."

The dirty ground had to be cleaned up first, which delayed the building. When it opened in 1990, it was called Carousel Center, named after a real carousel from 1908 that still turns inside the mall.

Later, the owners renamed it Destiny USA and grew it into a giant. By 2021, more than 26 million people were visiting each year.

So a pile of old scrap turned into one of the busiest malls in America.

6. 🌡️ The first fully indoor mall was built for Minnesota's cold

Southdale Center in Edina, Minnesota, opened in 1956. It was the first fully enclosed shopping mall in the US with climate control. Its roof and air system kept it at 75°F all year, like a spring day even in a blizzard.

Its architect, Victor Gruen, grew up in Vienna and wanted a town square where people would drink coffee and chat. He even imagined it would one day have schools, a medical center and homes.

Not everyone loved it. The famous architect Frank Lloyd Wright visited and said Gruen "should have left downtown, downtown."

In other words, the first indoor mall was born with a bad review.

7. 🛶 A Las Vegas mall has gondola rides on its second floor

The Grand Canal Shoppes are inside the Venetian resort in Las Vegas. The whole mall sits on the second floor, above the casino.

And yet it has a canal. Gondolas, the long boats of Venice, Italy, carry people past the shops. The ceiling is painted to look like the sky, and the main square copies St. Mark's Square in Venice.

It opened in 1999, but only 16 of its 72 shops and restaurants were ready on day one.

Think of it as a boat trip without ever going outside.

8. 🗿 Robot Roman gods put on shows at a Las Vegas mall

The Forum Shops at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas is built on land that used to be a car racing track. When it opened in 1992, it had a free show every hour: talking robot statues of Roman gods, like Bacchus and Venus, in a $4 million fountain.

By the time that show ended in 2011, the robots glitched a lot, and some people couldn't understand what they were saying.

Another robot show, added in 1997, got a makeover in 2013. In "The Fall of Atlantis," a king's children fight over the city of Atlantis until it sinks, with real fire and water. Next to it is a 50,000-gallon fish tank with 500 tropical fish, and underneath is a workshop where technicians keep the robots going.

A mall where the statues talk back.

9. 🏛️ America's first indoor mall now has tiny apartments

The Westminster Arcade in Providence, Rhode Island, opened in 1828, nearly 200 years ago. It's called the first enclosed shopping mall in the US: three floors of little shops under one long skylight.

At first, shoppers were slow to come, because it was far from the other stores. People nicknamed it "Butler's Folly," after its builder, Cyrus Butler. Things only changed when a fancy hat shop opened next door and pulled in rich shoppers.

Its front columns are 45 feet tall and were hauled 8 miles over dirt roads by oxen. Since 2013, it's been a mix of shops and micro-lofts, which are very small apartments.

America's oldest mall became a place to live.

10. ⛽ LA's tiny mini-malls grew where gas stations died

The tiniest malls are strip malls: a short row of shops with parking out front. The smallest start at about 5,000 square feet, about the size of a basketball court. The US has more than 68,000 of them.

In Los Angeles, they're called mini-malls. In 1973, an oil crisis made many gas stations go broke, leaving empty corner lots all over the city. Builders filled them with a few shops and a little parking lot.

The first modern one opened in Panorama City that year, and more than 600 followed. There got to be so many that in 1988, Los Angeles passed a rule to limit them.

A whole mall that fits where one gas pump used to stand.

Fun fact

Before Mall of America was built, its developers first dreamed up a giant indoor lake for the same spot, with 6-foot waves for indoor surfing and submarines to show visitors sea life. That plan was dropped. Decades later, the same company planned American Dream's water park around what would be the world's largest indoor wave pool, about 1.5 acres and 1.5 million gallons of water. Fake oceans, man-made waves and indoor beaches around the world are a whole wild world to explore next.
